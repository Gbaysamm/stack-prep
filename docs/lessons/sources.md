# Where subtopic lessons come from: the Nigerian source map

Every subtopic lesson follows how Nigerian secondary schools teach the topic (founder, 12 Nov
2026). This is where writers look, and what each source is for. Researched and checked
12 Nov 2026 for Chemistry, Biology and Mathematics; other subjects are added as they start.
Everything is used for approach, order, terms and common mistakes, in our own words: the
WAEC reports and the textbooks are copyright, and nothing is reproduced from them.

## The syllabus: JAMB first

- **JAMB UTME syllabus**, the order and wording we teach to. Official: IBASS,
  https://ibass.jamb.gov.ng (open in a browser; it is a script-driven app). Readable mirror:
  https://syllabus.ng/jamb/chemistry, /biology (its year labels disagree with themselves:
  check against IBASS). Our curriculum tables already follow it.
- **WAEC and NECO** (mirrors only: https://syllabus.ng/waec/chemistry/,
  https://syllabus.ng/neco/chemistry). Differences that matter: WAEC Chemistry starts with
  the atom and has practicals; JAMB starts with separation of mixtures and has none. JAMB
  Mathematics includes calculus. **Follow JAMB's topic names and order, not WAEC's.**
- **NERDC** curriculum documents are not online; a revised subject list is rolling out from
  SS1 (Sept 2025).

## What students get wrong: WAEC Chief Examiner's reports

https://waeconline.org.ng/e-learning/index.htm (Chemistry/chemmain.html,
Biology/biomain.html, Mathematics/mathsmain.html; 2008 to 2023). Each paper's "weaknesses"
page names the mistakes. These feed every lesson's **JAMB traps** section. Examples:

- Chemistry: no sign on oxidation numbers; writing a formula where a name is asked; weak
  IUPAC naming; stoichiometry; wrong unit for molar mass; unbalanced equations; the chemistry
  of HNO₃, H₂SO₄ and Cl₂; not relating chemistry to everyday life.
- Biology: misspelt names (Swann for Schwann); diagrams without titles; somatic vs
  reproductive cells; classifying organisms; plant nutrients and their deficiency symptoms.
- Mathematics: reading from a drawn graph; rounding too early; leaving out units; bearings,
  angles of elevation and depression, circle geometry; leaning on the calculator and leaving
  out working.

## How it is taught: the textbooks JAMB recommends

- Chemistry: Ababio, *New School Chemistry*; Bajah et al., *Senior Secondary Chemistry*;
  Ojokuku, *Understanding Chemistry*; Odesina, *Essential Chemistry*.
- Biology: Ramalingam, *Modern Biology*; Ndu, Ndu and Aina, *Senior Secondary School
  Biology*; Ogunniyi et al., *Biology for Senior Secondary Schools*; Stone and Cozens.
- Mathematics: Channon and Smith, *New General Mathematics for West Africa*; David-Osuagwu,
  *New School Mathematics*; Anyebe, *Basic Mathematics*; Tuttuh-Adegun, *Further Mathematics
  Project*.

## Notes and forums

1. Federal e-learning portal, https://elearn.education.gov.ng (topics with objectives): scope.
2. myschool.ng classroom (JAMB past questions with discussion threads): what confuses
   students. Answers are not vetted.
3. passnownow.com, schoolings.org, edupadi.com, teststreams.com (teacher notes, week by
   week): how schools sequence a topic. Quality varies; check every fact.
4. Nairaland JAMB tutorial threads: real confusions (older).

**Do not rely on:** lessonotes.com (has factual errors, e.g. phosphorus in group 16),
textbook-list blogs (they disagree), Telegram answer channels.

## Rules for writers (added to the lesson guide)

1. Name compounds the Nigerian way, with Roman-numeral oxidation states: carbon(IV) oxide,
   tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid, trioxonitrate(V) acid. The older name once, in brackets.
2. Oxidation numbers always carry their sign (+2, not 2); a name is never answered with a
   formula.
3. Units stay with every quantity through the working, and rounding happens only at the end.
4. Every step worked by hand: JAMB's CBT allows only a basic on-screen calculator.
5. Biology: names and terms spelt right; every diagram titled and labelled.
6. Mathematics: extra care on bearings, elevation and depression, circle geometry and
   reading a graph, modelled step by step.
7. JAMB's topic names and order.
8. Tie each idea to an everyday Nigerian example where it fits (cassava fermentation, a fire
   extinguisher), after the worked examples, not instead of them.
